What Changed in 2023, and How to Read a Climb-Up Listing

Since 2023, climbing onto Borobudur's terraces needs a separate timed ticket with a capped daily quota. You also need a local guide and woven upanat sandals, which protect the original stone steps. The ordinary courtyard ticket lets you walk the grounds and see the monument from below. It does not let you climb. The climb ticket gives access to the temple terraces. The monument has nine stacked platforms, six square and three circular, with 72 perforated stupas on the circular terraces.

Two mistakes to avoid are arriving without a climb booking and treating Setumbu Hill as part of Borobudur. Because the terraces work on a capped quota, a climb you have not booked may already be sold out when you reach the gate. Setumbu is a separate viewpoint about 4 km west of the temple, with its own ticket. A sunrise photo from the hill gives you a view of Borobudur, not a visit to its terraces.

So when I read a listing, I look for the words “climb to the top”, or something just as plain, in the description itself. A photo of stupas at dawn does not count. Then I look at the exact verb the listing uses for the climb, which I quote in each tour's section below. If a listing says nothing about the climb, ask which admission tickets are included before booking.

Why do I care so much about the climb? Around 2008, two years into my guiding, my father took me to the hidden base where the covered Karmawibhangga reliefs sit. He had guided Borobudur for thirty years. He put my hand on the stone and said the carvers buried these panels of cause and consequence on purpose, that some lessons are meant to hold up the whole structure from underneath. Until then I had been rattling off dates and measurements like a schoolboy. That morning I understood the temple is an argument about how to live. You follow that argument by walking the terraces upward and reading the reliefs clockwise, the way the builders intended. From the lawn you only see its outline.

Practical Notes: Tickets, Timing and What to Bring

Borobudur opens daily around 06:30 and closes around 16:30, and the terrace climb runs in capped timed slots. If you are arranging the visit yourself rather than through a tour, book the climb ticket online days ahead, not at the gate. Travellers who turn up hoping to climb often leave with a courtyard ticket only. Check current hours and ticket rules on the official Borobudur Park site.

You need the upanat sandals and a local guide on the terraces. If you are visiting both Borobudur and Prambanan within 48 hours, ask about the combined ticket. It is cheaper than two separate foreigner tickets, and operators rarely mention it. On an all-inclusive tour, check that you are not paying for tickets twice.

The Setumbu gate opens around 04:00, and in the dry season you want to arrive before 05:00. From the car park it is a short walk uphill. On a clear dry-season morning I would skip the hill and photograph from the terraces, where the light hits the stupas directly. Save Setumbu for when you want the misty silhouette with the volcano behind it. The dry season runs roughly May to September, and sunrise tours are most reliable then. My page on the best time to visit Yogyakarta has more detail.

Borobudur is roughly 40 km northwest of Yogyakarta city. Before any pre-dawn pickup, eat something, because nothing out there is open at that hour. Bring a light layer for the early drive.

What to Check Before Booking

  • Plain climb wording The listing should name the climb ticket in plain words, such as “climb to the top.” “Borobudur visit” or a sunrise photo is not enough.
  • Confirm the climb slot Ask the operator to confirm your climb slot and what happens if it cannot be secured or the day's quota is already full.
  • Climb time and temple order Get your climb time in writing. Ask whether the operator may switch the order of the temples depending on ticket availability.
  • Which rate option Confirm which rate option you are booking: VIP temple sunrise, Setumbu Hill sunrise, or no sunrise.
  • Setumbu or the temple If sunrise is listed, check whether it is watched from Setumbu Hill or from the temple itself. They are separate sites with separate tickets.
  • Pickup and vehicle Confirm the pickup time, the location and whether the car is private or shared, especially when the listing leaves pickup blank.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you still climb to the top of Borobudur?

Yes, but since 2023 you need a separate timed climb ticket with a capped daily quota, plus a local guide and upanat sandals. The ordinary courtyard ticket does not include the terraces.

Does a Borobudur sunrise tour include the climb?

Not automatically. A Setumbu Hill sunrise takes place about 4 km west of Borobudur, at a separate site with its own ticket. Check that the listing names the climb ticket. The Setumbu, Merapi and Prambanan listing on this page says all of its options include it.

Can I just buy the climb ticket at the gate?

You can try, but the daily quota is small and fills early. If you arrive without a booking, you may only get the courtyard ticket. Book online days ahead, or pick a tour whose listing states the climb ticket is included.

Is Borobudur, Merapi and Prambanan in one day too much?

It can be done, but it is a 12-hour march with a lot of driving between the sites, and it is easy to end up rushing Borobudur. If the terraces are your priority and you tire easily, choose one of the temple-only days.
