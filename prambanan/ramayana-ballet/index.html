Open-Air or Indoor: Which Theatre You Will Actually Get

The open-air Trimurti theatre has the lit temple as its backdrop. The dancers perform in front of the floodlit temple, and the tallest tower behind them is the Shiva temple, about 47 m high. It only runs in the dry season, roughly May to October. In the wet months the ballet moves to the covered Trimurti indoor theatre, which has no temple behind the stage. If a listing just says "Ramayana Ballet at Prambanan", it could mean either venue.

In the shoulder season of 2022 I told a honeymoon couple that the open-air ballet with the lit temple behind was worth the gamble. Of course a storm rolled in twenty minutes before the show. The performance moved to the covered theatre and I braced for their disappointment. But up close, the dancers' faces and the gamelan filled the room, and the wife told me she would have missed all that detail from the open-air seats. I learned to stop apologising for the indoor stage.

My advice is simple. If the lit temple behind the dancers is your reason for going, book a dry-season date and confirm the venue with the operator before you pay. If you are going for the dance and the music, either theatre will serve you well. My guide to the best time to visit Yogyakarta explains how the seasons affect the rest of your plans.

The Story Behind the Show, for First-Timers

The ballet draws on the Ramayana epic, which is also depicted along the inner balustrades of Prambanan's Shiva and Brahma temples. The full-day listing makes the same link, saying the performance takes its story from the reliefs at Prambanan. That gives you an easy way to prepare: see the carvings in daylight, then watch the same epic on stage that evening.

If you visit the temple first, give yourself time at the Shiva temple. It is the principal shrine of a complex dedicated to the Trimurti of Shiva, Vishnu and Brahma, and at about 47 m it is the tallest structure in the complex. The temples were built in the 9th century, around 850 CE, and the site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. The 2006 Yogyakarta earthquake damaged several structures, and restoration has continued in phases since, so expect scaffolding on parts of the complex.

Read a short outline of the Ramayana before the evening so you can follow the performance. Pair that with a slow walk past the balustrade reliefs, and the stage and the stone become two tellings of one story.

Practical Notes: Tickets, Timing and Getting Home

The ballet ticket is separate from temple entry. Ballet seats are priced in tiers by seating position, and a daytime visit to Prambanan needs its own ticket. If you are buying admission independently and visiting both temples within 48 hours, the combined ticket costs less than separate foreigner tickets. Check your package's inclusions before buying additional tickets. Prambanan opens daily around 06:30 and closes around 17:00. The show starts around 19:30.

For the temple itself, go in from the back. Walk north to Sewu temple first, while the tour buses crowd the main Shiva temple, then circle back. You get the headline temple with thinner crowds an hour later. In the wet season, visit in the morning. Afternoon storms are typical, and the open site has little shelter.

If you are planning an independent visit, arrange your return transport before you go. Book a tour that confirms return transport, or agree a return pickup time and meeting point with a driver in advance, so you are not searching for a ride once the show ends. My guide to getting around Yogyakarta covers your options. Bring a rain jacket in the wet months.

What to Check Before Booking

  • Which theatre Confirm whether your date is at the open-air stage or the indoor theatre, especially if the temple backdrop is why you are going.
  • Ballet actually included Make sure the ballet is named in the listing. The private temple day does not mention it.
  • Pickup and return Check where you are picked up and who drives you home after the show, because the listings shown here vary on pickup detail.
  • Time inside Prambanan For an evening tour, ask how long you get in the temple grounds before the complex closes around 17:00.
  • Length of day Be honest about whether you can handle a 720-minute day before booking the full combination.
  • Tickets covered Ask whether temple entry and ballet seats are both included, and which seating tier you get.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Ramayana Ballet at Prambanan held outdoors?

The venue depends on the season; see the theatre guidance above.

What time does the Ramayana Ballet start?

The show starts around 19:30. If you want to see the temple first, arrive in the afternoon, because the complex closes around 17:00.

Is the ballet ticket included with Prambanan temple entry?

No. Ballet seats are a separate ticket, priced by seating tier. Confirm whether your chosen package includes temple entry and ballet admission, and which seating tier is covered.

Is the indoor show worth it in the wet season?

Yes. During my 2022 visit, the dancers' expressions and the gamelan stood out indoors. If you came for the performance, the indoor theatre serves you well.

How do I get back to Yogyakarta after the show?

Confirm return transport with your tour operator, or book a driver in advance with an agreed pickup time.
