If you can spare two mornings, give each temple its own morning, and read the ticket notes in the practical section below.

How the Two Temples Actually Differ

Prambanan is Hindu, and it is built upward. It dates from the 9th century, around 850 CE, and it is the largest Hindu temple complex in Indonesia. It is dedicated to the Trimurti: Shiva, Vishnu and Brahma, with Shiva as the principal deity. The Shiva temple rises about 47 m. The original complex held 240 temples, and many are now ruins around the rebuilt centre. The inner balustrades of the Shiva and Brahma temples carry the Ramayana in carved relief, the same story the evening ballet tells. Expect some scaffolding, because the 2006 earthquake damaged several structures and restoration has continued in stages since then. Prambanan also has a Buddhist neighbour. Sewu Temple, a large Buddhist complex, is within walking distance just to the north, inside the same archaeological park.

Borobudur is Buddhist, and it asks something different of you. The listings below offer access to climb Borobudur to the top. Reaching the top now depends on a separate climb ticket, and two of the listings below mention a limit of 1,200 climbers per day. Do not count on walking up without one. I explain how climb tickets work, and which tours actually secure them, in my guide to Borobudur climb-up tours with guaranteed access. One listing offers a sunrise option from Setumbu Hill.

In the dry season of 2017, at the top of Setumbu Hill, a retired German couple asked me the question this page is about. They had booked me partly because I speak conversational German. The husband asked me to name the finest temple in Java. Out of habit I started to say Borobudur, then I stopped. I told him it depends what you came to feel: Prambanan reaches up like a prayer, and Borobudur asks you to walk inward. He nodded and said he trusted me more for not selling him a winner. After that we watched the mist lift off the Kedu plain in silence.

That is still my answer. If you want height, drama and a story you can follow on the walls, lean towards Prambanan. If you want a slow, inward climb and you are willing to get up early, lean towards Borobudur. If you can manage both, see both.

Practical Notes: Timing, Tickets and Getting There

Prambanan is about 17 km east of Yogyakarta city. InJourney's Prambanan guidance, dated 29 July 2026, lists ticket counter hours of 06:30 to 17:00 WIB and temple area hours of 07:00 to 17:30 WIB. The same guidance says that on Mondays the Brahma, Shiva and Vishnu main temple areas close for maintenance while the courtyard stays open. Go in the morning, especially in the wet season, roughly November to March. The site has little shelter, so a wet-season morning visit helps avoid the typical afternoon storms. More Prambanan-focused options are on my Prambanan tours page.

If you are paying entry separately, the official Prambanan Free Borobudur package lists both temples' entrance tickets, but its page does not say whether the Borobudur part includes climbing, how long it stays valid, or what it saves against two single tickets. Check those terms, and what your tour already includes, before buying it. For Borobudur, the official structure ticket page lists climb hours of 08:30 to 17:00 WIB and includes the courtyard, a guide and upanat sandals, while the courtyard-only ticket does not include climbing and is listed for domestic visitors only. For current opening hours, check the official Borobudur Park site rather than relying on third-party claims.

To avoid crowds at Prambanan, start from the back. Walk to Sewu first thing while the tour buses crowd the Shiva temple. When you come back to the main temple an hour later, the crowds are thinner. On a group tour, ask your guide whether the schedule allows this.

Sunrise tours are most reliable in the dry months. My guide to the best time to visit covers the seasons in more detail. If you fly into Yogyakarta International Airport, remember it is about 40 km west of the city, around 1-1.5 hours by road. Do not plan a long temple day for the day you land. For sunrise starts, check the forecast and bring suitable layers and rain protection.

What to Check Before Booking

  • Ticket inclusion Check whether the price includes temple entry and the Borobudur climb ticket. Some listings say they do, and the ClimbUP listing says it does not.
  • Order of the day Ask which temple comes first. One listing says the order depends on ticket availability, which matters if you want a particular temple in the morning.
  • Ballet venue and seats If the Ramayana Ballet is part of the plan, find out whether it is the open-air or indoor venue that night and which seating tier is included.
  • Pickup area and time Confirm that your hotel is inside the pickup zone and get the exact pickup time, especially for sunrise options.
  • Time at each stop On three-stop days, ask the operator about stop order and how long you get at each temple.
  • Two-temple package If you are paying entry yourself, check whether the official Prambanan Free Borobudur package includes the Borobudur climb before choosing it over a structure ticket.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Borobudur or Prambanan better?

Neither is better. They were built to do different things. Prambanan is a cluster of Hindu spires with the Ramayana carved on its walls, and Borobudur is a Buddhist temple you can climb to the top with the right ticket. Choose by what you want to feel, or see both.

Can you visit Borobudur and Prambanan in one day?

Yes. All four tours on this page combine them, with listed durations between 480 and 720 minutes. The Borobudur, Merapi and Prambanan listing runs for 720 minutes. Check the listed duration and confirmed sunrise arrangements for your chosen option.

Do I need to book the Borobudur climb in advance?

Check climb-ticket availability before making plans: listings mention a limit of 1,200 climbers per day. Choose a tour that explicitly secures access or check the official Borobudur Park site.

Is Prambanan worth visiting in the rainy season?

Yes, if you go in the morning. Afternoon storms are common in the wet season and the site has little shelter. The Ramayana Ballet also moves to the indoor theatre in those months, which still makes a good evening.
